# Italy: Customer-Acquisition Channel Economics and Outreach Law

Research date: 2026-08-13. Scope: indie founder selling self-serve micro-SaaS to Italian micro and small businesses.

**Confidence convention.** `high` = primary/official source or multiple independent credible sources. `medium` = single credible source with disclosed methodology. `low` = single secondary source, undisclosed methodology, or a figure derived from another market.

**Staleness convention.** Two different things are flagged differently:
- `[STALE-DATA]` — a benchmark or measurement whose underlying data is older than 12 months. This is a real quality problem.
- `[IN-FORCE]` — a legal instrument adopted more than 12 months ago that is still binding law. Age is not a defect here. The 2013 Garante guidelines are from 2013 and are still the operative rule; they are not "outdated".

---

## 1. Cold outreach law: the hard rule

### 1.1 Bottom line

**Cold email to Italian company addresses, and cold PEC in particular, is not legally safe for an indie founder.** Both are prohibited without prior opt-in consent. The rule is statutory (art. 130 co. 1-2, d.lgs. 196/2003), it is not softened by the recipient being a business, and it is not satisfied by the address being publicly available. The Garante has issued a prohibition order over PEC scraping from INI-PEC (2018) and a €20,000 fine over the same practice (2021). PEC is treated as an *aggravating* factor, not a neutral channel.

Three specific beliefs an indie founder is likely to hold, and why each is wrong:

| Belief | Status |
| --- | --- |
| "The address is public in INI-PEC / Registro Imprese, so I can use it" | Wrong. Public availability is expressly rejected as a basis. Confidence: high |
| "It's a company address, so GDPR/spam rules don't apply" | Wrong twice: `nome.cognome@azienda.it` is a personal address, and legal persons are still protected as *contraenti* under art. 130 | 
| "I'll include an unsubscribe link, so it's fine" | Wrong. Consent must precede the first message. Confidence: high |

### 1.2 Primary rule: prior consent, public sources excluded

Garante, *Linee guida in materia di attività promozionale e contrasto allo spam*, provv. n. 330 of 4 July 2013, doc. web 2542348, published in Gazzetta Ufficiale n. 174 of 26 July 2013, §2.5. `[IN-FORCE]` Confidence: high.

> «Ai trattamenti effettuati ai fini promozionali tramite strumenti automatizzati o a questi equiparati si applica l'art. 130, commi 1 e 2, del Codice, in base al quale l'utilizzo di tali strumenti per le finalità di marketing è consentito solo con il consenso preventivo del contraente o utente (c.d. opt-in). Quindi, ai fini della legittimità della comunicazione promozionale effettuata, non è lecito, con la medesima, avvisare della possibilità di opporsi a ulteriori invii, né è lecito chiedere, con tale primo messaggio promozionale, il consenso al trattamento dati per finalità promozionali.»

> *"Processing carried out for promotional purposes through automated means or means equivalent to them is subject to art. 130(1) and (2) of the Code, under which the use of such means for marketing purposes is permitted only with the prior consent of the subscriber or user (so-called opt-in). Therefore, for the promotional communication to be lawful, it is not permissible to use that same communication to give notice of the possibility of objecting to further sendings, nor is it permissible to request, with that first promotional message, consent to data processing for promotional purposes."*

Same paragraph, on public sources, which is the sentence that kills list-building from registries:

> «senza il consenso preventivo […] non è possibile inviare comunicazioni promozionali con i predetti strumenti neanche nel caso in cui i dati personali siano tratti da registri pubblici, elenchi, siti web atti o documenti conosciuti o conoscibili da chiunque.»

> *"Without prior consent […] it is not possible to send promotional communications by the aforesaid means even where the personal data are drawn from public registers, directories, websites, records or documents known or knowable by anyone."*

And, specifically on PEC:

> «Analogamente, senza il consenso preventivo degli interessati, non è lecito utilizzare per inviare e-mail promozionali gli indirizzi pec contenuti nell'"indice nazionale degli indirizzi pec delle imprese e dei professionisti" […] istituito per favorire la presentazione di istanze, dichiarazioni e dati, nonché lo scambio di informazioni e documenti tra la pubblica amministrazione e le imprese e i professionisti in modalità telematica.»

> *"Likewise, without the prior consent of the data subjects, it is not lawful to use, for sending promotional e-mails, the PEC addresses contained in the 'national index of PEC addresses of businesses and professionals' […] established to facilitate the submission of applications, declarations and data, and the exchange of information and documents between the public administration and businesses and professionals by electronic means."*

Note the statutory basis is `art. 130, commi 1 e 2` of d.lgs. 196/2003 (Codice privacy), which implements the ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC art. 13. Verbatim from Gazzetta Ufficiale (annotations to DPR 26/2022):

> «1. […] l'uso di sistemi automatizzati di chiamata o di comunicazione di chiamata senza l'intervento di un operatore per l'invio di materiale pubblicitario o di vendita diretta o per il compimento di ricerche di mercato o di comunicazione commerciale è consentito con il consenso del contraente o utente. […] 2. La disposizione di cui al comma 1 si applica anche alle comunicazioni elettroniche, effettuate per le finalità ivi indicate, mediante posta elettronica, telefax, messaggi del tipo Mms […] o Sms […] o di altro tipo.»

> *"1. […] the use of automated calling or call-communication systems without human intervention for sending advertising material or for direct selling or for carrying out market research or commercial communication is permitted with the consent of the subscriber or user. […] 2. The provision in paragraph 1 also applies to electronic communications, made for the purposes indicated therein, by e-mail, fax, MMS […] or SMS […] or other type."*

### 1.3 The B2B question: does it help that the recipient is a company?

No, and the reason is two-layered. Linee guida 2013, §2.2. `[IN-FORCE]` Confidence: high.

**Layer 1 — a named company mailbox is a personal address.**

> «Può essere questo il caso dei dipendenti che lavorano in una determinata società che ha fornito loro indirizzi di posta elettronica aziendale contenenti le loro generalità (ad esempio, nome.cognome@società.com). Ebbene […] tali indirizzi vanno considerati indirizzi "personali" di posta elettronica e i loro rispettivi assegnatari come "interessati", con la conseguente applicabilità del Codice e del relativo impianto di diritti e tutele.»

> *"This may be the case of employees working at a given company that has provided them with corporate e-mail addresses containing their personal details (for example, firstname.lastname@company.com). Such addresses must be regarded as 'personal' e-mail addresses and their respective holders as 'data subjects', with the consequent applicability of the Code and its framework of rights and protections."*

**Layer 2 — even for a pure legal person, art. 130 still applies.** Since 6 December 2011 (d.l. 201/2011 art. 40 co. 2) legal persons are no longer *interessati* and cannot file complaints with the Garante, but they remain *contraenti* and therefore remain within the scope of art. 130:

> «i detti soggetti, in quanto "contraenti", si possono avvalere degli ordinari strumenti di tutela forniti dall'ordinamento, quindi, possono esperire presso l'autorità giudiziaria ordinaria rimedi civilistici quali, ad esempio, l'azione inibitoria e/o l'azione di risarcimento del danno oppure, eventualmente qualora ricorrano gli elementi costitutivi dell'art. 167 c.p., anche sporgere denuncia […]. Inoltre, essi possono beneficiare dell'eventuale esercizio dei poteri di iniziativa ex officio -quanto a provvedimenti inibitori, prescrittivi e/o sanzionatori- da parte di questa Autorità.»

> *"Such subjects, as 'subscribers', may avail themselves of the ordinary remedies provided by the legal system, and may therefore bring before the ordinary courts civil remedies such as, for example, an injunction and/or a claim for damages or, where the constituent elements of art. 167 of the Criminal Code are present, also file a criminal complaint […]. Furthermore, they may benefit from the exercise of ex officio powers — as regards prohibitory, prescriptive and/or sanctioning measures — by this Authority."*

So the practical exposure for a founder emailing `info@` addresses of Italian SRLs is: civil injunction and damages from the recipient, plus Garante enforcement on its own initiative. The absence of a complaint route does not make it lawful.

### 1.4 Legitimate interest vs consent

For electronic direct marketing (email, PEC, SMS, fax, automated calls) the debate is closed in Italy in practice: **art. 130 is lex specialis and requires consent; legitimate interest under GDPR art. 6(1)(f) is not an available basis for this channel.** Confidence: high for the operative conclusion, based on how the Garante actually decides cases.

The clearest demonstration is the Isinc decision (§1.5): the company argued that the addresses were freely available online and that an unsubscribe link was provided. The Garante did not weigh a legitimate-interest balancing test at all. It applied art. 130 and found the violation established, citing both art. 130 of the Codice and art. 6(1)(a) GDPR (consent) as breached.

The one statutory carve-out is **"soft spam"**, art. 130 co. 4, referenced in Linee guida §2.7 and in the Isinc decision:

> «potendosi ammettere una deroga unicamente nel caso in cui l'indirizzo email sia stato rilasciato dall'interessato nel contesto di una vendita di beni o servizi analoghi.»

> *"a derogation being admissible only where the e-mail address was provided by the data subject in the context of a sale of similar goods or services."*

For a micro-SaaS founder this means: emails collected from your own customers, about similar products, with opt-out available, are lawful. Emails to people who have never bought from you are not, regardless of framing.

### 1.5 Enforcement: the actual decisions and amounts

**Isinc S.r.l.s. — ordinanza ingiunzione, 21 April 2021, provv. n. 149, doc. web 9680996. €20,000 fine.** Confidence: high (primary). This is the decision to cite in the pack, because the facts are exactly the indie-founder scenario at small scale.

Facts: lawyers complained about promotional emails sent to PEC addresses "verosimilmente estratte dal registro INI-PEC" (likely extracted from the INI-PEC register). The company's defence was that the message carried a full privacy notice, that the address "era liberamente disponibile on-line" (was freely available online), and that recipients could unsubscribe via a link or by writing to privacy@isinc.it.

Outcome and the aggravating factor that matters most:

> «deve altresì rilevarsi che proprio l'utilizzo dello strumento della posta elettronica certificata rende il trattamento idoneo ad arrecare maggiori interferenze all'esercizio dell'attività professionale degli interessati e per tale ragione risulta più grave»

> *"it must also be noted that the very use of the certified e-mail (PEC) instrument makes the processing liable to cause greater interference with the data subjects' exercise of their professional activity, and for that reason it is more serious."*

> «il grado di responsabilità del titolare del trattamento, da qualificarsi come gravemente colposo, tenuto conto che la Società ha inviato messaggi promozionali senza consenso ritenendo sufficiente la presenza degli indirizzi in un registro pubblico, nonostante il chiaro disposto dell'art. 130 del Codice e nonostante le innumerevoli pronunce rese negli anni dal Garante da considerarsi ormai ampiamente note»

> *"the degree of responsibility of the controller, to be qualified as grossly negligent, considering that the Company sent promotional messages without consent believing the presence of the addresses in a public register to be sufficient, despite the clear wording of art. 130 of the Code and despite the countless rulings issued over the years by the Garante, now to be considered widely known."*

Being a microenterprise was counted as a *mitigating* factor ("la natura di microimpresa della Isinc, da poco sul mercato"), and the €20,000 was set with explicit reference to limiting the economic impact on a small company. So €20,000 is the *reduced* figure for a first-offence microenterprise; it can be halved to €10,000 under art. 166 co. 8 by settling within 30 days. The statutory maximum under GDPR art. 83(5) is €20m or 4% of worldwide turnover.

**Mevaluate Italia S.r.l. / Associazione Mevaluate onlus — provv. n. 52 of 1 February 2018, doc. web 7810723. Prohibition and erasure order; no fine in this decision.** Confidence: high (primary). Correcting a likely assumption: this decision is a *provvedimento inibitorio e prescrittivo*, not an ordinanza-ingiunzione. The Garante explicitly deferred the monetary question:

> «l'Autorità si riserva di valutare, con autonomo procedimento, la sussistenza dei presupposti per contestare, all'Associazione e alla Società, le violazioni amministrative concernenti la violazione degli artt. 13, 23 e 130 del Codice»

> *"the Authority reserves the right to assess, in a separate proceeding, whether the conditions are met to charge the Association and the Company with the administrative violations concerning breach of arts. 13, 23 and 130 of the Code."*

I could not find the outcome of that separate proceeding (see §7).

The scale and the mechanics are still the useful part. Volume: 840,348 PEC messages sent as of 10 October 2016, harvested against ~240,000 lawyers, ~117,000 accountants/auditors, ~27,000 labour consultants and ~6,000-7,000 notaries. Collection method: «mediante varie modalità di tipo manuale e di tipo automatizzato con software di raccolta automatica dei dati» (*"by various manual and automated methods, with automatic data-harvesting software"*) from INI-PEC, registroimprese.it and professional-association PDF listings.

Two independent grounds, both of which apply to any scraper:

1. **Purpose limitation on INI-PEC.** The register exists «per favorire la presentazione di istanze, dichiarazioni e dati, nonché lo scambio di informazioni e documenti tra la pubblica amministrazione e le imprese e i professionisti» (*"to facilitate the submission of applications, declarations and data, and the exchange of information and documents between the public administration and businesses and professionals"*), per art. 6-bis CAD. INI-PEC's own legal notice page repeats the prohibition.

2. **Bulk extraction is reserved to public administrations.** Art. 16 co. 10, d.l. 185/2008: individual lookups are free, but «l'estrazione di elenchi di indirizzi è consentita alle sole pubbliche amministrazioni per le comunicazioni relative agli adempimenti amministrativi di loro competenza» (*"the extraction of lists of addresses is permitted only to public administrations for communications relating to administrative obligations within their competence"*), «con conseguente esclusione, pertanto, della liceità della medesima operazione da parte di soggetti privati» (*"with the consequent exclusion of the lawfulness of the same operation by private parties"*).

So scraping INI-PEC is independently unlawful *before* you send anything, regardless of message content — the decision says the extraction is unlawful «peraltro indipendentemente dal contenuto» (*"moreover irrespective of the content"*).

The decision also disposes of the "it's from other public registers so it's fine" fallback (§7):

> «la necessità del previo consenso informato dell'interessato sussiste anche quando i dati personali […] siano rinvenibili in altri registri o elenchi pubblici (quali quelli disponibili sul sito www.registroimprese.it o sui siti web istituzionali degli ordini provinciali delle categorie professionali), in quanto l'agevole reperibilità degli stessi non ne autorizza il trattamento per qualsiasi scopo, ma soltanto per le specifiche finalità sottese alla loro pubblicazione»

> *"the need for the data subject's prior informed consent exists also where the personal data […] are findable in other public registers or lists (such as those available on www.registroimprese.it or on the institutional websites of the provincial professional associations), because their ready availability does not authorise their processing for any purpose whatsoever, but only for the specific purposes underlying their publication."*

And on unsubscribe links (§8): the presence of a removal link does not cure the illegality, because consent «deve essere legittimamente acquisito anteriormente all'invio delle comunicazioni promozionali» (*"must be lawfully obtained prior to sending the promotional communications"*).

**Criminal exposure.** Failure to comply with a Garante prohibition order is punishable under art. 170 of the Codice with «la reclusione da tre mesi a due anni» (*"imprisonment from three months to two years"*). This is quoted in both decisions. It attaches to ignoring the order, not to the original spam.

### 1.6 The asymmetry that matters for distribution: phone is opt-out, email is opt-in

This is the single most actionable legal finding for the distribution pack, and it points the opposite way from most founders' instincts.

Art. 130 co. 3-bis of the Codice privacy, verbatim from Gazzetta Ufficiale. `[IN-FORCE]` Confidence: high.

> «3-bis. In deroga a quanto previsto dall'articolo 129, il trattamento dei dati di cui al comma 1 del predetto articolo, mediante l'impiego del telefono e della posta cartacea per le finalità di invio di materiale pubblicitario o di vendita diretta o per il compimento di ricerche di mercato o di comunicazione commerciale, è consentito nei confronti di chi non abbia esercitato il diritto di opposizione […] mediante l'iscrizione della numerazione della quale è intestatario […] in un registro pubblico delle opposizioni.»

> *"3-bis. By way of derogation from art. 129, the processing of the data referred to in paragraph 1 of that article, by means of the telephone and paper mail for the purposes of sending advertising material or direct selling or carrying out market research or commercial communication, is permitted in respect of anyone who has not exercised the right to object […] by registering the number of which they are the holder […] in a public register of objections."*

Linee guida §2.5 states the same permission positively: it *is* permitted to call, with a human operator, numbers **present in public telephone directories** and not registered in the RPO, in order to ask the subscriber for consent to receive promotional communications by the art. 130(1)-(2) channels.

**Practical consequence: telephone and paper mail are opt-out channels in Italy; email, PEC, SMS and automated calls are opt-in.** A founder who wants first contact with a business that has never heard of them has a lawful path by phone and by post, and no lawful path by email.

**Do not over-read this permission — it is narrower than "cold calling is legal in Italy".** Art. 130 co. 3-bis is drafted as a derogation from **art. 129**, which governs the data of *contraenti* appearing in publicly available telephone directories (*elenchi cartacei o elettronici a disposizione del pubblico*). The carve-out therefore covers numbers that are in those directories. A micro-business switchboard number harvested from the company's own website, from Registro Imprese, or from a scraped business directory is not obviously an art. 129 number, and if it is not, co. 3-bis does not authorise the call — leaving the founder back at needing an ordinary GDPR art. 6 basis for the processing. I could not resolve this point from a Garante or MIMIT statement (see §7 items 9 and 13).

Operating rule for the pack: treat the phone carve-out as available for **directory-listed numbers, RPO-screened**, and treat website-scraped or registry-scraped numbers as unresolved rather than green-lit.

The compliance cost of the phone route is real but bounded:
- The RPO was extended to **all national numbers including mobiles from 27 July 2022** (governo.it, 2022-10-25; DPR 26/2022). Confidence: high.
- Operators must **register with the RPO and consult it monthly, and in any case before each campaign** (l. 5/2018 art. 1 co. 12). Confidence: high.
- Screened lists are **valid 15 days for telephone, 30 days for paper mail**. Confidence: high (MIMIT operator documentation).
- Access is a **prepaid annual subscription banded by list size**, set by ministerial decree. The 2025 tariffs were extended to 2026 by MIMIT notice of 23 December 2025 pending the new decree of 22 December 2025. I did not retrieve the euro amounts per band (see §7). Confidence: high on the mechanism, none on the price.

Caveat I could not resolve: whether a *company's* switchboard number is protected by the RPO on the same footing as a natural person's number. The RPO is framed around the *contraente telefonico*, which formally includes legal persons, and the safe operating assumption for the pack is that business numbers must be screened too.

### 1.7 One profession-specific trap

If the ICP is **commercialisti**, note that their own code of conduct forbids *them* from doing what the founder might be tempted to do, and the norm signals how the audience reads unsolicited pitches. CNDCEC Codice deontologico art. 44 co. 2, in force since 1 April 2024, via IPSOA (2024-08-02):

> «è in particolare vietato inviare, anche tramite terzi, comunicazioni telematiche e messaggi elettronici a potenziali clienti, offrendo le proprie prestazioni professionali senza che questi ne abbiano fatto richiesta»

> *"it is in particular forbidden to send, including through third parties, electronic communications and electronic messages to potential clients, offering one's professional services without their having requested it."*

Confidence: medium (secondary source quoting the code verbatim; I did not retrieve the CNDCEC original).

### 3.3 Structural headwind: zero-click

One relevant Italy/Europe figure: around **60% of searches in Europe do not lead to clicks on external sites** (hangler.it, 2025-11-18, citing zero-click growth). If accurate, published keyword volumes overstate reachable traffic substantially, which compounds the thin-long-tail problem.

### 4.2 Professional-publisher sponsorships: no public rate cards

I checked Euroconference, IPSOA (Wolters Kluwer) and the FiscoeTasse/Euroconference News cluster. **None publishes a media kit, rate card, or "advertise with us" page that I could retrieve.** Italian B2B professional publishers quote on request. That is the finding; it is not a data gap I can close from public sources.

**A structural warning that is more useful than a price would have been.** Euroconference — the dominant training and daily-news publisher for Italian commercialisti, consulenti del lavoro and avvocati, operating Euroconference News / ecnews.it and the FiscoPratico platform — states on its own site that it is **"Gruppo Euroconference Spa, società di TeamSystem"** (euroconference.it, undated; confidence: high, vendor primary). TeamSystem is the incumbent Italian management-software vendor. Sponsoring the main professional-audience channel therefore means paying an incumbent competitor for access to that incumbent's own audience, and it means any sponsorship is subject to a channel owner with a direct commercial interest in the same buyers. Verify the current ownership before acting, but plan on the assumption that the obvious "sponsor the commercialisti newsletter" play routes budget to a competitor.

### 4.4 A tax note the pack should carry

Advertising and sponsorship spend is **fully deductible** as *spese di pubblicità* under art. 108 TUIR when there is a synallagmatic contract (payment against a defined promotional counter-performance); *spese di rappresentanza* are only partially deductible and carry non-recoverable VAT. Cassazione ord. 5317/2025 requires the taxpayer to document that the promotional service was actually performed — contract and invoice alone are insufficient; screenshots, event programmes and press records are expected. Source: IPSOA, 2026-02-27 and 2025-11-03. Confidence: medium. Practical effect for the CAC model: keep sponsorship contracts specific about deliverables and retain proof of delivery, or the effective cost rises by the lost deduction and VAT.

## 5. Reseller and intermediary channel: how Italian micro-firms buy software

### 5.1 The commercialista is a real gatekeeper, but a narrow one

The best available research is the **Osservatorio Professionisti e Innovazione Digitale, Politecnico di Milano School of Management**, 2023/24 edition, presented 4 July 2024. `[STALE-DATA]` — the fieldwork is ~2 years old, though the structural findings are unlikely to have reversed. Confidence: high (named research programme, method disclosed as an online questionnaire, sponsor list published including CNDCEC, AIDC, ANCL).

| Finding | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Share of PMI collaborating externally on digitalisation that name professional studios as an interlocutor | **34%** |
| Nature of that support | "prevalentemente l'attività amministrativa e, meno, gli aspetti di pianificazione/consulenza" — mostly administrative work, less planning/advisory |
| Share of PMI *not* using professionals for digital projects who considered them unsuitable for the topic | **over 80%** |

Read carefully, this cuts both ways and the second number is the more important one. A third of digitalising Italian PMI do route through their professional studio, so the channel is real. But over 80% of the rest actively judged the commercialista *not* a credible interlocutor on digital projects. The Osservatorio's own conclusion is that this reveals "ampi margini di crescita" — i.e. the gatekeeper role is potential, not established.

**Implication for a micro-SaaS founder:** a commercialista-referral channel works when the product sits inside the administrative workflow the studio already owns (accounting, payroll, e-invoicing, document exchange). It does not work as a generic recommendation engine for business software, because the client base does not grant the studio authority in that domain.

### 5.2 What the studios themselves run, and where the gaps are

Same source, 2023/24. Technology penetration among **commercialisti**:

| Technology | Penetration |
| --- | --- |
| Accounting software | 88% |
| E-invoicing | 87% |
| Video calling | 73% |
| Compliant digital preservation (*conservazione digitale a norma*) | 37% |
| eLearning platforms | 35% |
| Client document-sharing portal | 31% |
| CRM, business intelligence, AI, workflow | at or near the bottom, "al massimo nel 50%" for everything outside the verticals |

Among **consulenti del lavoro**: payroll 94%, e-invoicing 82%, contract management 74%, VPN 49%, website 44%, advanced technologies 1-8%.

Digital spend, 2023: €1.888bn total across lawyers, commercialisti and labour consultants (+7% YoY), forecast €1.982bn for 2024 (+5%). Average per-studio: multidisciplinary €25,100, labour consultants €12,900, commercialisti €12,100, lawyers €9,500. In the >€10k spend band: 3% of micro-studios, 33% of small, 67% of medium, 87% of large.

The distribution read: the vertical core is saturated by incumbents, and the open space is in the 31-37% band (client portals, document preservation, eLearning) and in the near-zero band (CRM, workflow, AI). But note the micro-studio spend profile — only 3% of micro-studios spend over €10k/year on technology — which is the segment most structurally similar to a micro-SaaS price point and the least likely to buy.

### 5.3 Stated barriers, which double as objection-handling

The Osservatorio reports that for commercialisti, labour consultants and multidisciplinary studios the third-ranked concern for the future is «la difficoltà di far percepire l'utilità di nuovi servizi alla clientela» — the difficulty of making clients perceive the usefulness of new services. The first two are staffing and generational succession. A founder pitching a studio as a reseller is therefore pitching into an organisation whose stated problem is that its own clients don't buy new things.

## 6. Synthesis for the packs

**For the CAC calibration pack.** Italian paid acquisition is genuinely cheaper than US benchmarks — best-evidenced at ~52% below global median CPC on Meta, and directionally 1.5-3x cheaper than US on Google search for comparable B2B intent — but the discount is smaller than the market-size discount. The channel to model is Google Search at €1.50-€4.00 CPC for B2B/professional-services intent with a 2-5% conversion rate, and Meta at ~€0.43 CPC (from a ~$0.50 median) against a thin decision-maker pool. LinkedIn should be modelled as unavailable below a €2,000/month floor and a €207 CPL. Apply a Milan/Rome premium of roughly 20-50% and a Q4 Meta CPM spike of 30-50%. Treat every Google Ads Italy figure as a planning range, not a measurement: no Italy-cut study with disclosed methodology exists.

**For the distribution pack, the hard rule.** No cold email. No cold PEC. No scraping INI-PEC or Registro Imprese for outreach lists — the extraction is independently unlawful before a single message is sent. The only lawful email marketing is to your own customers about similar products (soft spam, art. 130 co. 4). Precedent for getting this wrong as a microenterprise: €20,000, halvable to €10,000 by prompt settlement.

**The compliant bridge from first touch to a mailing list.** This is the operational answer to "so how do I ever email anyone in Italy?" Linee guida §2.5 expressly permits calling a **directory-listed, RPO-screened** number with a human operator *for the purpose of asking the subscriber for consent* to the art. 130(1)-(2) channels. So the lawful sequence is: phone call → consent obtained and documented → email marketing thereafter. Paper mail is available on the same opt-out footing. RPO compliance for the phone leg means registering as an operator, consulting the register monthly and before each campaign, and re-screening lists every 15 days. Do not extend this to numbers scraped from company websites or Registro Imprese — see the scope limit in §1.6.

**Strongest alternative channels found, in order of evidenced viability.** (1) SMAU Milano, at €4,500+IVA or free via regional de minimis programmes for startup/PMI innovative, with a concrete lead-capture mechanism. (2) Content/SEO against the Italian long tail, instrumented with SEOZoom — Evolution at €76/month (annual billing) is the cheapest tier the 2026 pricing page lists as including API access, though the vendor's 2024 API guide says all tiers include it, so confirm before choosing on that basis — accepting that the demand cannot be validated from published research and must be measured first. (3) Commercialista referral, but only for products inside the administrative workflow the studio already owns, and against a stated 80%+ credibility gap outside it.

## 7. Does not exist publicly

Items I searched for and could not find. None of the below should be invented or estimated in the packs.

1. **An Italy-cut Google Ads benchmark with disclosed methodology.** WordStream's annual study is explicitly US-only. Every Italy CPC table found is either an agency's undisclosed internal account sample or openly self-described as US benchmarks adapted to Italy. The Italy figures in §2.2 are all `low` confidence for this reason.

2. **A current (post-2020) measurement of Italy's CPC discount versus the US.** The only country-comparison study located is WordStream's, whose underlying data is from 2015 despite a 2025 "last updated" stamp.

3. **Any published volume-distribution study of Italian-language long-tail software or SaaS queries.** Neither SEOZoom nor Semrush publishes one. Italian long-tail SaaS demand appears to be unmeasured in public research.

4. **Semrush's published Italian database size.** Not located.

5. **The outcome of the separate sanctioning proceeding the Garante reserved against Mevaluate** in provv. 52/2018 §11. If a fine was issued, I did not find it. Do not state that the 2018 Mevaluate decision carried a fine — it did not.

6. **Euro amounts for the RPO annual subscription bands.** The mechanism, the banding-by-list-size structure, and the 2026 tariff extension are all confirmed from MIMIT sources, but the tariff table itself is inside DM 20 December 2024 / DM 22 December 2025, which I did not retrieve in full.

7. **Media kits, rate cards or sponsorship pricing for FiscoeTasse, Euroconference, IPSOA** or comparable Italian professional publishers. These are quote-on-request.

8. **Any pricing for Italian business-niche podcast or YouTube sponsorships.** No aggregator or published rate card found.

9. **A definitive answer on whether a company's main switchboard number is protected by the RPO identically to a natural person's number.** The framing around *contraente* suggests yes; I could not confirm it from a Garante or MIMIT statement.

10. **Published research on the software *purchase process* of Italian micro-firms specifically** (evaluation steps, decision-maker, trial behaviour). The Osservatorio work covers professional studios' own technology adoption and their advisory relationship with PMI, not the PMI purchase funnel. AssoSoftware's "Il software gestionale in Italia" research (with Osservatori Digital Innovation) exists but I retrieved only a 2022 announcement, not the data. `[STALE-DATA]`

11. **Any data on incumbent agent/reseller network economics in Italy** (margins, coverage, terms for a micro-SaaS vendor joining one). Not found publicly.

12. **"Google Ads grants alternatives" for Italian for-profit micro-SaaS.** Google Ad Grants is nonprofit-only; no equivalent Italian public scheme granting ad credit to for-profit software startups was found. The functional equivalent that does exist is the regional de minimis fair-participation funding documented in §4.1.

13. **Whether art. 130 co. 3-bis covers a business number sourced outside public telephone directories** — from the company's own website, Registro Imprese, or a scraped business directory. The provision is drafted as a derogation from art. 129, which is about directory-listed data, so the carve-out may not reach these numbers at all. No Garante or MIMIT statement resolving this was found. This is the single most consequential open legal question in the report, because it determines how wide the one lawful cold-contact channel actually is.
