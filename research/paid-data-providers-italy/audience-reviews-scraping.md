Scope: web/traffic intelligence with Italy cuts, consumer review streams (Trustpilot IT), social/community signal at scale (Reddit, LinkedIn groups, Facebook/Telegram existence checks), and Italian audience/panel data. ## Per-provider notes

### Apify
Apify's platform pricing (apify.com/pricing, observed 2026-08-14) is a subscription-plus-usage model: Free ($5/mo credit) → Starter $29/mo → Scale $199/mo → Business $999/mo, each tier unlocking a lower compute-unit rate ($0.20 → $0.16 → $0.13/CU) and Store discounts (Bronze/Silver/Gold) on participating actors. Most of the actors we need run on **pay-per-event (PPE)** pricing, which bypasses the compute-unit math entirely — you're billed a fixed price per result, with compute usually bundled into that price. This makes cost forecasting straightforward: a specific run's cost is `(events) × (price per event)`, independent of how long the actor takes to run.

For the four target workloads, the market has several competing actors at different price/quality points; naming the best-maintained ones (based on 2026-updated dates, feature completeness, and price competitiveness observed on 2026-08-14):

- **App reviews (Apple App Store + Google Play)**: `automation-lab/apple-app-store-scraper` ($0.60–1.15/1K reviews depending on plan tier, no subscription) and `solidcode/google-play-store-reviews-scraper` ($0.40/1K reviews) are both actively maintained, pay-per-event, and support country-code filtering. `andok/app-store-reviews` is a viable alternative bundling both stores in one actor.
- **Reddit**: `blackfalcondata/reddit-scraper` is explicitly positioned (per its own store listing, updated May 2026) as the cheapest full-featured option at $1.25/1,000 results + $0.005/run, built specifically to survive Reddit's May 2026 shutdown of the public `.json` endpoint that broke older scrapers. `automation-lab/reddit-scraper` offers steep volume discounts (down to $0.28/1,000 posts at "Diamond" tier). Both operate outside Reddit's official OAuth API — see the Reddit ToS negative-log entry below for the legal caveat.
- **LinkedIn posts/groups**: `scraping_solutions/linkedin-posts-scraper-users-groups-schools-no-cookies` explicitly supports groups at $25/month rental + usage. Cheaper per-post options exist for *profile* posts only (e.g. `harvestapi/linkedin-profile-posts` at $1.50–2.00/1,000) but don't cover groups specifically — for our community-signal use case, the rental actor is the one that actually reaches group content.
- **Trustpilot**: `automation-lab/trustpilot` at $0.20/1,000 reviews (Playwright-based, handles Trustpilot's AWS WAF JS challenge, 30 fields/review) is the cheapest full-data option observed. As of April–May 2026 several actor listings (`harvestlab/trustpilot-scraper`) explicitly flag Trustpilot as intermittently blocking automated access even through residential proxies ("Watchlist status — May 2026"), so budget for occasional failed/retried runs and treat any single provider's success rate as unstable rather than guaranteed.
- **Telegram**: `automation-lab/telegram-scraper` ($0.001/message via public `t.me/s/` preview pages, no auth needed) covers channel existence/activity checks; `opportunity-biz/telegram-group-scraper` ($1/1,000 members) goes further into supergroup member extraction but needs a user session string, which raises its own ToS exposure.
- **Facebook**: no dedicated actor for group-existence checks turned up in this search round; see negative log.

Realistic monthly cost for our stated workloads (a few hundred app reviews, a few thousand Reddit posts/comments, a couple thousand LinkedIn group posts, and continuous Trustpilot IT monitoring for one to a handful of brands) lands comfortably inside the Apify Free plan's $5/month credit for occasional runs, and inside a $29/month Starter plan for regular monitoring — the per-event actor prices above are all cents-to-low-dollars per 1,000 results.

### Similarweb
Similarweb replaced its older "hits-based" API pricing with a **Data Credits** model (docs.similarweb.com/api-v5/guides/data-credits-calculations, page timestamped 2026-08-13). There is no public per-dollar price list at all — API access is sold as an add-on to Business/Enterprise platform subscriptions or as a sales-quoted standalone package (similarweb.com/corp/daas/api/; similarweb.com/packages/web/). A third-party synthesis (scrapegraphai.com/blog/similarweb-api-pricing, 2026-07-08) confirms there's no self-serve checkout — every price is a sales conversation.

Independent of price, Similarweb's own accuracy documentation (support.similarweb.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002219177, and the "why does the API return exact values for small sites" article) states plainly that sites under 5,000 monthly visits get suppressed/unreliable estimates, and a third-party 2026 review (syncgtm.com/blog/similarweb-review-2026, 2026-06-10) puts variance at 50–200%+ for anything under 100K monthly visits — which describes almost every indie product and most Italian SMB competitor sites we'd actually want to research. Combined with the lack of a self-serve tier, **Similarweb API is not viable for a solo dev or for our workload**; it only makes sense sales-led, for large-scale competitor sets with material traffic.

### Trustpilot official Business API
Trustpilot's own developer platform (developers.trustpilot.com) splits into two products: the **Business API** (for businesses managing their own reviews — invitations, replies, widgets) and the separate **Data Solutions API** (Display/Insights, for reading *other* companies' review data). The Business API's API Module is only available as an add-on on the Premium ($799/mo/domain) and Enterprise tiers (business.trustpilot.com/pricing, observed 2026-08-14) — pricing for the add-on itself isn't published. The Data Solutions API, which is the one that would actually give us competitor/market review data, requires joining a waitlist (developers.trustpilot.com/data-solutions-get-started) with no published pricing or approval timeline. **There is no legitimate affordable route to bulk IT review data through Trustpilot's official channels** — Apify scraping (at $0.20–0.50/1,000 reviews) remains the practical option, understanding it operates against Trustpilot's own terms-of-service language on "commercial redistribution" (business judgment call, not legal advice).

### Reddit official Data API
Multiple independent 2026 sources (prowlo.com, snitchfeed.com, businesscloud.co.uk, scavio.dev — all observed 2026-08-14, published between June–July 2026) converge on the same numbers: the free tier is capped at 100 queries/minute per OAuth client, requires manual approval since Reddit's "Responsible Builder Policy" closed self-service registration in late 2025, and is explicitly **non-commercial only** — Reddit's own policy (redditinc.com/policies/data-api-terms and support.reddithelp.com's commercial-use article) lists "services, research, or data access for fees" as commercial use, which almost certainly captures our market-validation workflow. Commercial access starts around $12,000/month for 50M calls (~$0.24/1,000 calls), with no middle tier. This confirms the free tier is not usable for anything resembling our product, and the commercial tier is wildly disproportionate to our need. Apify's third-party Reddit scrapers are the practical answer, at the cost of relying on tools that work by scraping rather than using the sanctioned API (several actor listings note they had to adapt after Reddit shut down the unauthenticated `.json` endpoint in May 2026, which is itself a signal that this route could break again without warning).

### Audience panels: GWI, YouGov, Comscore
None of the three offers an affordable single-country API tier:
- **GWI** has a genuine self-serve dashboard tier (Plus, ~$150/user/month per gwi.com/pricing) but that's dashboard-only access, not an API — API/MCP access is gated to the Pro tier, which is custom-quoted (confirmed via GWI's own docs at api.globalwebindex.com/docs, which state pricing comes from "your Account Manager").
- **YouGov** publishes pricing for commissioning *new* self-service surveys (~$1–15+ per completed response, yougov.com/business/products/self-serve-surveys/price-calculator), which is a different product from licensing the existing Profiles/BrandIndex panel data via API — that access is entirely contract-gated with no public number (confirmed indirectly via Vendr's 2026 buyer's-guide summary, a third-party source, not YouGov's own page).
- **Comscore** publishes no pricing anywhere in its public materials; every product (Data Feeds, Digital Analytix, Plan Metrix) is sales-quoted. Its Online Product Terms PDF confirms Italy is one of the measured geographies for cross-platform audience metrics, but that's a feature confirmation, not a price.

All three are **enterprise-only, skip** for a solo-dev/indie budget. If Italy audience-composition data is genuinely needed at some point, the AGCOM Osservatorio (free, see below) is a usable free proxy for platform-level unique-user and time-spent figures, sourced from these same panels at one remove.

### SerpApi vs. Exa for Google.it SERP observation
SerpApi's public pricing (serpapi.com/pricing, observed 2026-08-14) runs $25/month (1,000 searches, $25/1K) up to $275/month (30,000 searches, $9.17/1K), with an Enterprise tier starting at $3,750/month base plus $2.75–$30/1,000 on top depending on speed tier. A third-party comparison (apiserpent.com/blog/serpapi-pricing-explained, 2026-07-17) notes SerpApi has no pay-as-you-go option — unused searches expire monthly — and lines up pay-as-you-go alternatives (DataForSEO $0.60–2.00/1,000; Serpent API from $0.60/1,000, no subscription) that are meaningfully cheaper for bursty or low-volume use. SerpApi does support Google.it targeting via standard `gl`/`hl` parameters — no special Italy pricing or limitation was found. Given we already pay for Exa, **SerpApi is worth evaluating only if a specific run needs true Google SERP scraping** (features Exa doesn't replicate, e.g. exact ranking position, People Also Ask boxes, or local pack results) rather than as a standing subscription.

### Italy-specific findings
- **Audiweb** is the AGCOM-recognized Joint Industry Committee for Italian web-audience measurement. Its published rate card (audiweb.it/come-aderire/aziende.html) runs €28,500–€346,000/year, positioned for publishers, agencies, and large advertisers — not indie research.
- **Auditel/Audicom** (Italy's TV and now cross-media "Ricerca Integrata" panel, which absorbed Audiweb's role from January 2026) publishes an actual rate card (auditel.it, "listino tv digital 2026" PDF) with fixed annual fees from €1,000–€8,000/year for software houses and information-service companies, scaling up via revenue bands or ad-spend-linked variable tariffs for media agencies and rights-holders. Interesting as a benchmark that even the *cheapest* legitimate tier of Italian audience-panel access starts around €1,000–3,000/year — confirms there's no genuinely low-cost official entry point to panel-level Italian audience data.
- **AGCOM's own Osservatorio pubblications** (quarterly "Osservatorio sulle Comunicazioni" and the newer annual "Osservatorio sul sistema dell'informazione") are free, public PDFs that already aggregate Comscore/Auditel/Audiweb data into usable Italy-specific benchmarks: e.g., the 1-2026 Osservatorio (published 2026-05-14) reports Reddit Italy at 11.8M unique users in December 2025 (+38.1% YoY), LinkedIn at 14.1M (+1.6%), and general internet news consumption stats. These are a legitimate free substitute for expensive panel access when the need is "how big is platform X in Italy," not granular targeting data.
- No AGCOM/Audicom self-serve API or affordable single-purchase data product was found; all identified access routes are either free published reports or five-to-six-figure annual licenses.

## Ranked recommendation

**Worth paying for now** (already fits our Exa + Apify budget, no new contract needed):
1. Apify pay-per-event actors for app-store reviews, Reddit, Trustpilot IT, and Telegram — cents-to-low-dollars per 1,000 results, fits inside the existing Free/Starter Apify plan for our stated workload.
2. AGCOM Osservatorio public reports — free, Italy-specific, good for sanity-checking platform-scale claims.

**Pay only when a specific run needs it** (don't subscribe standing, spin up per-task):
1. Apify's LinkedIn groups actor ($25/mo rental — only activate for the runs that need it, then pause).
2. SerpApi, only if a task needs true Google.it SERP scraping beyond what Exa returns; DataForSEO or Serpent API are cheaper pay-as-you-go alternatives worth a look first.
3. GWI Plus ($150/user/month self-serve dashboard, no API) — only for occasional ad hoc Italy audience-composition lookups, not for automation.

**Enterprise-only, skip** (no realistic self-serve path for our scale):
1. Similarweb API (sales-quoted, and its own accuracy docs disqualify it for the small-site traffic tier we'd query).
2. Trustpilot official Business/Data Solutions API (Premium+ add-on or enterprise waitlist; Apify scraping is the practical substitute).
3. Reddit official commercial Data API ($12K/month floor; free tier explicitly excludes commercial/monitoring use).
4. YouGov Profiles/BrandIndex API, Comscore (all products) — no public pricing exists at all.
5. Audiweb, Auditel/Audicom respondent-level panels — €1,000–€346,000/year tariff bands, built for publishers/agencies, not indie validation.
